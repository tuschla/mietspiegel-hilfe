„§“ means section (Paragraf, part of a law), „Rn.“ means Randnummer (margin number, a paragraph in a judgment).

## Does this page apply to your letter?

Only if you are asked to agree to a higher rent (consent, Zustimmung; the letter often says „Zustimmung“, „Mieterhöhungsverlangen“ (rent increase demand) or „§ 558 BGB“).

**Other rules** apply to an increase after a modernisation (§ 559 BGB), to new advance payments for service charges (Betriebskosten) (§ 560 BGB), to index rent or graduated rent (Indexmiete, Staffelmiete; § 557b, § 557a BGB; as long as it is agreed, an increase under § 558 BGB is excluded) and in old social housing with “cost rent” („Kostenmiete“; the letter then often also says „Wirtschaftlichkeitsberechnung“ (economic viability calculation) or „Wohnungsbindungsgesetz“). You then usually have to pay the higher rent even without consent.

**No statutory rules on rent increases** apply to a room furnished by the landlord in the flat where the landlord lives (unless you live there permanently with your family or partner), to flats let only for temporary use (a contract with an end date is not enough for this on its own) and to rooms in a student or youth hall of residence (Studenten- oder Jugendwohnheim). There your contract counts, and the landlord can give notice more easily.

The Berlin rent index (Mietspiegel) does not apply to flats in one- or two-family houses and terraced houses, flats with the toilet outside the flat, or flats that only became ready for occupancy (bezugsfertig) after its period (Mietspiegel 2026: from 2025; Mietspiegel 2024: from 2023). This can also be a new flat in an old building, for example after a loft conversion, an extension or the conversion of commercial premises. In a one- or two-family house or terraced house, the landlord may still justify the increase with the Mietspiegel, at least if the rent demanded lies within the range for multi-family buildings; the letter is then not invalid for that reason alone. But the Mietspiegel does not show whether the rent is usual for your type of house.

In newer social housing and other subsidised flats without “cost rent” (Kostenmiete), the landlord must ask for your consent as described here; in addition, the rent must not exceed the maximum rent set by the subsidy, and the Mietspiegel cannot be applied directly.

In these cases, do **not** simply stop paying. Get advice: [Help and advice](@/hilfe.en.md).

*Source: § 557a Abs. 2, § 557b Abs. 2 BGB; § 10 Wohnungsbindungsgesetz; § 28 Abs. 3 Wohnraumförderungsgesetz; Senatsverwaltung, Berliner Mietratgeber „Zulässige Miethöhe“ (new social housing); § 549 Abs. 2 und 3 BGB; Berliner Mietspiegel 2026, Nr. 3 und 6.4; BGH, Urteil vom 17.09.2008, VIII ZR 58/08; BGH, Beschluss vom 26.04.2016, VIII ZR 54/15, Rn. 8 bis 12.*

## What is a rent increase demand?

Under § 558 BGB, the landlord may not raise the rent himself but must ask for your consent: at least in text form (Textform, for example a letter, fax or email) and with reasons, for example the Mietspiegel. If he uses other reasons, he must still state the values of the Berlin Mietspiegel if it contains values for your flat. He can still correct formal errors, such as missing reasons, during a court case; your time to think it over then starts again.

*Source: § 558, § 558a Abs. 1 und 3, § 558b Abs. 3 BGB.*

## Which Mietspiegel applies?

The usual rent is determined for the day the letter reaches you (receipt, Zugang). Write down this day and keep the envelope. Usually the Mietspiegel of that day applies: for receipt from 28.05.2026 the Mietspiegel 2026, from 30.05.2024 to 27.05.2026 the Mietspiegel 2024.

**Note if the letter arrived from 01.09.2025 to 27.05.2026:** Because the Mietspiegel 2026 is based on rents as of 01.09.2025, a court may also use it or add a surcharge. Calculate with both Mietspiegel editions and get advice.

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2021, VIII ZR 22/20, Rn. 29 (day of receipt); LG Berlin, Urteil vom 07.11.2014, 65 S 527/13 (newer Mietspiegel); BGH, Urteil vom 15.03.2017, VIII ZR 295/15 (surcharge).*

## The deadlines

| Deadline | Rule | Example: receipt on 15 October |
|---|---|---|
| Your time to think it over | until the end of the second calendar month after the month of receipt | 31 December |
| New rent if you agree | from the start of the third calendar month after receipt, or from a later date the landlord names | 1 January |
| Landlord’s claim if you do not agree or only agree in part | until three months after the end of your deadline; after that he can no longer sue for this increase | 31 March |

If a deadline ends on a Saturday, Sunday or Berlin public holiday (for example New Year’s Day, Women’s Day on 8 March, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, 3 October, the two Christmas holidays), the next working day usually takes its place. For your time to think it over, this is what the law says; we have not found a court decision on exactly this point. Plan for your reply to arrive by the calendar date. The deadline to sue moves in the same way: if by calculation it ends on Sunday, 28.02.2027, a claim that reaches the court by Monday, 01.03.2027, and is served soon afterwards is enough. It is not settled whether the deadline for special termination (see below) moves; do not count on it.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 1 und 2 BGB; § 193 BGB; § 167 ZPO; Berliner Gesetz über die Sonn- und Feiertage, § 1; BGH, Urteil vom 29.04.2020, VIII ZR 355/18 (deadline to sue); BGH, Versäumnisurteil vom 06.12.2007, III ZR 146/07, Rn. 13 und 14 (claim filed on the Monday after a deadline ending on a Saturday was in time).*

## Silence is not consent

If you do nothing, the landlord can sue; the court then checks whether the increase is justified. **Post from the local court (Amtsgericht)?** Go to an advice service at once. You often have only 2 weeks to reply to the court; otherwise it can decide without you, usually in favour of the landlord.

**Be careful when paying:** Consent does not need any particular form. Paying the higher rent several times without reservation (ohne Vorbehalt) can also count as consent; the Federal Court of Justice (Bundesgerichtshof) saw it this way for three payments. So do not pay the higher rent as long as you do not want to agree. If the landlord collects the rent by direct debit (Lastschrift), check your account. If the higher rent is collected before you have agreed, write to the landlord immediately that you have not agreed and are only paying under reservation (unter Vorbehalt). Ask your bank whether you can reverse the direct debit, and get advice.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 2 BGB; BGH, Beschluss vom 30.01.2018, VIII ZB 74/16, Rn. 13 und 21; § 276 Abs. 1, § 331 Abs. 3 ZPO.*

## Agree, agree in part, refuse

**Consent is final.** Once it has reached the landlord, you can neither take it back nor cancel it like an online purchase. The new rent then counts as agreed, as a rule even if you notice later that it is too high. It can be challenged (anfechten) only in exceptional cases, for example if the landlord deliberately deceived you; in that case, get advice immediately.

*Source: § 130 Abs. 1 BGB, § 123 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 17.10.2018, VIII ZR 94/17; confirmed in the Urteil vom 24.04.2019, VIII ZR 62/18, Rn. 21 und 22.*

**Tenancy agreement concluded in June 2015 or later?** Before you reply, check whether your starting rent was already too high under the [rent brake (Mietpreisbremse)](@/mietpreisbremse.en.md). After a consent you can, as a rule, no longer rely on the rent brake against the new rent.

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 28.09.2022, VIII ZR 300/21, Leitsätze (headnotes) and Rn. 32.*

**Agreeing in part** is also possible: the law says that „soweit“ (to the extent that) you agree, you owe the higher rent, for example €30 more instead of the €60 more demanded. You are bound by this, and the landlord can still sue for the rest. So only agree to the amount you are sure is correct, and afterwards only pay the rent you agreed to, not the full amount demanded. If you pay by direct debit, check your account.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 20.01.2010, VIII ZR 141/09, Rn. 19; BGH, Beschluss vom 30.01.2018, VIII ZB 74/16, Rn. 21.*

**Refusing:** you do not have to give reasons. But you can name features that you see differently; get advice first.

If, as often happens, the letter comes with a form from the landlord to sign, only sign it if you understand everything on it. Our letter templates only concern the rent increase and nothing else in your tenancy agreement.

## The limits of a rent increase

The new rent must not exceed the lower of two limits:

1. **The local comparative rent (ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete),** that is, the usual rent for similar flats. In Berlin this is usually the value from the Mietspiegel, placed in the range with the Orientierungshilfe (orientation guide). Work it out with our [calculator](@/rechner.en.md).
2. **The cap (Kappungsgrenze):** In Berlin, the net cold rent (Nettokaltmiete, without service charges and heating costs) may rise by at most 15 % in three years, starting from the rent three years before the day from which the new rent is to apply. Increases after a modernisation (§ 559 BGB) do not count here and may come on top of the 15 %. Your advance payment for service charges (Nebenkosten) is not part of the net cold rent.

Example: three years ago the net cold rent was €600. The cap is then €690 (€600 + 15 %). If the comparative rent is €660, €660 is the limit. If the rent has since risen by €40 because of a modernisation, the cap is €690 + €40 = €730.

*Source: § 558 Abs. 1 bis 3 BGB; Berliner Kappungsgrenzenverordnung of 14.03.2023, valid until 10.05.2028 (published in the Gesetz- und Verordnungsblatt für Berlin 2023, S. 112); BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2004, VIII ZR 177/03.*

## The waiting periods

- **1-year blocking period (Sperrfrist):** A new demand may reach you one year after the day the last increase took effect at the earliest; otherwise it is invalid. If there has been no increase yet, the widely held view is that the year counts from the start of the tenancy, that is, from the day from which you had to pay rent under the contract. We have not found a decision of the Federal Court of Justice (Bundesgerichtshof) on this; get advice in that case.
- **15-month waiting period (Wartefrist):** The new rent may only apply once the rent has stayed the same for 15 months, counted from the day the last increase was first due (as a rule, the day it took effect); if there has been no increase yet, from the start of the tenancy.
- Increases after a modernisation and changes to service charges (§§ 559 bis 560 BGB) do not count for either period.

*Source: § 558 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 20.01.2010, VIII ZR 141/09, Rn. 18; BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2004, VIII ZR 178/03.*

## What counts for your flat?

- Only the landlord’s fittings count. What you installed at your own cost does not count, permanently, even if it replaced an old fitting of the landlord with permission. It is different if the landlord paid you back the costs.
- In our understanding, the condition of the flat on the day of receipt counts, because the usual rent is determined for that day. Take dated photos right afterwards.
- What counts is the real living space, not the number in the tenancy agreement. If you dispute the area with your own measurements, the landlord must prove the real area. If you think the number in the agreement is too large, get advice.

*Source: Berliner Mietspiegel 2026, Nr. 6.3; BGH, Urteil vom 24.10.2018, VIII ZR 52/18; BGH, Urteil vom 18.11.2015, VIII ZR 266/14 (Leitsatz a); BGH, Urteil vom 31.05.2017, VIII ZR 181/16, Rn. 13.*

## Who has to prove what?

In a court case, the landlord must prove the local comparative rent. For the features of the Orientierungshilfe, according to the Landgericht Berlin (Berlin Regional Court, today Landgericht Berlin II), the landlord must prove the plus features and you must prove the minus features you rely on. Other courts may see this differently. So collect evidence early: tenancy agreement, handover report (Übergabeprotokoll), dated photos, invoices for things you installed yourself, letters to the landlord, names of witnesses.

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 18.11.2020, VIII ZR 123/20, Rn. 27; LG Berlin, Urteil vom 16.10.2018, 67 S 150/18, Rn. 6 bis 9.*

## Terminate instead of agreeing (special right of termination, Sonderkündigungsrecht)

After a rent increase demand, you may terminate the tenancy with extraordinary notice (außerordentlich kündigen), that is, with a shorter notice period than usual; the increase then does not apply. The notice must reach the landlord by the end of the second month after receipt. The tenancy ends at the end of the month after next: if the notice arrives in December, it ends at the end of February. The law says „Monat“ (month) here, not „Kalendermonat“ (calendar month). You are on the safe side if the notice arrives within two months from the day of receipt.

The notice must be on paper with your signature; an email is not enough. If several people signed the tenancy agreement, as a rule all of them must sign. Get advice first.

*Source: § 561 BGB, § 568 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 16.03.2005, VIII ZR 14/04.*

## State-owned landlords

At the state-owned housing companies (landeseigene Wohnungsunternehmen) degewo, GESOBAU, Gewobag, HOWOGE, STADT UND LAND and WBM, additional commitments apply, mainly from their „Kooperationsvereinbarung“ (cooperation agreement) with the State of Berlin (01.01.2024 to 31.12.2027). For berlinovo it only applies to flats in the area „Klassisches Wohnen“ (classic housing) that berlinovo itself owns, not to the many older flats of its fund companies (Fondsgesellschaften). If your landlord is a subsidiary of one of these companies, or if you are unsure, ask the ombudsman office (Ombudsstelle, see below).

- **Still 11 % instead of 15 %:** For increases under § 558 BGB, the rent under one tenancy agreement should rise by at most 11 % in three years as long as the cooperation agreement runs. This is stated in the new version of the „Bündnis für Wohnungsneubau und bezahlbares Wohnen“ (Alliance for New Housing Construction and Affordable Housing) of 15.07.2025, to which the companies belong.
- **Limit in euros per flat:** In total, the monthly rent may rise by at most €50 (flat up to 65 m²), €75 (up to 100 m²) or €100 (up to 125 m²). For larger flats, the agreement does not name an amount, and it does not say expressly whether „insgesamt“ (in total) applies to each increase or to the whole term.
- „In Summe“ (in total), the companies keep rent increases to at most 2.9 % per year. This is an average across all their flats, not a limit for your flat.
- Under a Berlin law, the companies should („sollen“) raise the rent under § 558 BGB by at most 15 % in four years. „Sollen“ means: exceptions are possible; it is not a firm right for you. If your household is recognised as a hardship case (income at most 220 % of the limits under § 9 Abs. 2 WoFG; living space as below for the affordability promise), on application the rent after an increase should be at most 30 % of its net income, or at most 27 % if the energy certificate (Energieausweis) of your building shows a final energy consumption of more than 170 kWh per m² and year. According to the ombudsman office, this law does not apply to berlinovo.

**Affordability promise (Leistbarkeitsversprechen)** of the cooperation agreement: the net cold rent should be at most 27 % of the household income (according to the ombudsman office: net income). This only applies on your application (auf Antrag) and only if

- your income is not above the limits for a housing entitlement certificate (Wohnberechtigungsschein, WBS). The agreement does not say exactly which limit. The Berlin law on the state-owned companies names, for hardship cases, 220 % of the income limits from the federal Housing Promotion Act (Wohnraumförderungsgesetz, § 9 Abs. 2 WoFG). According to the ombudsman office, it must first be checked whether you can get housing benefit (Wohngeld).
- the flat is not too large: 45 m² for one person, 60 m² for two, 75 m² for three, 85 m² for four, 12 m² more for each additional person. Otherwise the rent is only reduced proportionally. According to the ombudsman office, in special circumstances up to 20 % more area can be accepted.

According to the ombudsman office, an informal letter or an email to your housing company is enough as an application, with proof of income and household size. If you receive basic income support for jobseekers (Grundsicherung für Arbeitsuchende, formerly Bürgergeld) or social assistance (Sozialhilfe; SGB II or XII), the agreement promises that the rent stays affordable for you. The companies must point out the affordability promise in every rent increase demand.

All these rules are voluntary commitments and „should“ rules (Soll-Regeln); whether and how they can be enforced in court is unclear. Get advice. Questions are answered by the Ombudsstelle für Mieterinnen und Mieter der landeseigenen Wohnungsunternehmen (ombudsman office for tenants of the state-owned housing companies): <https://www.ombud-lwu.de> (German and English).

*Source: Kooperationsvereinbarung 2024, Präambel und Abschnitt II Nr. 4, 7 und 8; Bündnis für Wohnungsneubau und bezahlbares Wohnen, Vereinbarung vom 15.07.2025; Gesetz zur sozialen Ausrichtung und Stärkung der landeseigenen Wohnungsunternehmen, § 2 Abs. 4 und § 3; Roadmap 2.0 von Land Berlin, landeseigenen Wohnungsunternehmen und berlinovo vom 05.06.2026; Ombudsstelle, Startseite und FAQ.*
