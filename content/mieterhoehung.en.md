This page explains the most important rules. It is general and does not replace advice in an individual case. Below each section you will find the source. With it, you or an advice centre can look everything up. Two abbreviations: **§** means section (Paragraf), that is, a part of a law. **Rn.** means Randnummer (margin number), that is, a paragraph in a judgment.

## Does this page apply to your letter?

This page only applies if the letter says that you should **agree** to a higher rent (consent, Zustimmung). The letter often says „Zustimmung“, „Mieterhöhungsverlangen“ (rent increase demand) or „§ 558 BGB“.

**Careful – other rules apply here.** In these cases you usually have to pay the higher rent, even without your consent:

- rent increase after a modernisation (§ 559 BGB),
- new advance payments for service charges (Betriebskosten) (§ 560 BGB),
- index rent (Indexmiete, § 557b BGB) or graduated rent (Staffelmiete, § 557a BGB),
- old social housing with “cost rent” (Kostenmiete). The letter then often says „Kostenmiete“, „Wirtschaftlichkeitsberechnung“ (economic viability calculation) or „Wohnungsbindungsgesetz“.

The rules on this page also do **not** apply to:

- a room in the flat where the landlord lives himself, if the landlord has furnished it (not if you live there permanently with your family or your partner),
- a flat that is only let for temporary use (a contract with an end date is not enough for this on its own),
- a room in a student or youth hall of residence (Studenten- oder Jugendwohnheim).

In these three cases, the statutory rules on rent increases do not apply. What applies depends on your contract. But the landlord can give you notice more easily there.

In addition, the Berlin rent index (Mietspiegel) does not apply to flats in one- or two-family houses and terraced houses, to flats with the toilet outside the flat, and to new buildings (Mietspiegel 2026: completed from 2025; Mietspiegel 2024: completed from 2023).

**Newer social housing or another subsidised flat without “cost rent” (Kostenmiete):** Here the landlord must ask you for your consent, as described on this page. In addition, the rent must not exceed the maximum rent set by the subsidy. The Mietspiegel cannot then be applied directly. Get advice.

In these cases, do **not** simply stop paying. Get advice: [Help and advice](@/hilfe.en.md).

*Source: § 10 Wohnungsbindungsgesetz; § 28 Abs. 3 Wohnraumförderungsgesetz; Senatsverwaltung, Berliner Mietratgeber „Zulässige Miethöhe“ (new social housing); § 549 Abs. 2 und 3 BGB; Berliner Mietspiegel 2026, Nr. 3.*

## What is a rent increase demand?

With a rent increase under § 558 BGB, your landlord may not simply raise the rent himself. He must ask you to agree. The limit is the rent that is usual for similar flats. This is called the local comparative rent (ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete). In Berlin it usually comes from the Mietspiegel.

The letter must at least be in text form (Textform), for example a letter, fax or email. The landlord must give reasons for the increase, for example with the Mietspiegel. If he uses other reasons, he must still state the values from the Berlin Mietspiegel for your flat.

If you have agreed a graduated rent or index rent, an increase under § 558 BGB is excluded during this time.

*Source: § 558, § 558a Abs. 1 und 3, § 557a Abs. 2, § 557b Abs. 2 BGB.*

## Which Mietspiegel applies?

What matters is the day the letter reaches you (receipt, Zugang). The usual rent is determined for this day. Usually the Mietspiegel that applies on that day is used:

- Receipt from 28.05.2026: Mietspiegel 2026.
- Receipt from 30.05.2024 to 27.05.2026: Mietspiegel 2024.

**Note if the letter arrived between 01.09.2025 and 27.05.2026:** The Mietspiegel 2026 is based on rents as of 01.09.2025. A court may therefore also use the Mietspiegel 2026 for this period. It may also add a surcharge. In that case, calculate with both Mietspiegel editions and get advice.

Write down the day the letter arrived. Keep the envelope.

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2021 – VIII ZR 22/20, Rn. 29 (day of receipt); LG Berlin, Urteil vom 07.11.2014 – 65 S 527/13 (newer Mietspiegel); BGH, Urteil vom 15.03.2017 – VIII ZR 295/15 (surcharge).*

## The deadlines

**Your time to think it over:** You have until the end of the second calendar month after the month in which the letter arrived. Example: the letter arrives on 15 October. Then you have until 31 December.

**From when the new rent applies:** If you agree, you pay the higher rent from the start of the third calendar month after receipt. In the example: from 1 January. The landlord may also name a later date. Then the later date applies.

**The landlord’s deadline:** If you do not agree, or only agree in part, the landlord can go to court and sue for consent. For this he has three more months after the end of your deadline. In the example: until 31 March. If he does not sue in time, he can no longer sue for this increase.

**Saturday, Sunday, public holiday:** If the end of a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday in Berlin, the next working day usually takes its place. Public holidays in Berlin are New Year’s Day, Women’s Day (8 March), Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, 3 October and the two Christmas holidays.

- **Your time to think it over:** According to the wording of the law, it moves to the next working day. We have not found a court decision on exactly this point. So plan for your reply to arrive by the calendar date.
- **The landlord’s deadline:** It also moves. The landlord then has more time. Example: by calculation the deadline to sue ends on Sunday, 28.02.2027. Then the landlord has until Monday, 01.03.2027. It is enough if the claim reaches the court by then and is served soon afterwards.
- **Special termination (see below):** It is not settled whether this deadline moves. Do not count on it.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 1 und 2 BGB; § 193 BGB; § 167 ZPO; Berliner Gesetz über die Sonn- und Feiertage, § 1; BGH, Urteil vom 29.04.2020 – VIII ZR 355/18 (deadline to sue); BGH, Versäumnisurteil vom 06.12.2007 – III ZR 146/07, Rn. 13 und 14 (claim filed on the Monday after a deadline ending on a Saturday was in time).*

## Errors in the letter

If the letter has formal errors, for example no reasons, the landlord can still make up for this or correct it during the court case. You then have the full time to think it over again.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 3 BGB.*

## Silence is not consent

If you do nothing, you have not agreed. But the landlord can then sue. A court then checks whether the increase is justified.

**Post from the court:** If the landlord sues, you will get post from the local court (Amtsgericht). Go to an advice service at once. You often have only 2 weeks to reply to the court. If you do not reply in time, the court can decide without you, usually in favour of the landlord.

**But be careful when paying:** Consent does not need any particular form. If you pay the higher rent several times without reservation (ohne Vorbehalt), this can count as consent. The Federal Court of Justice (Bundesgerichtshof) saw it this way for a payment made three times without reservation. So do not simply pay the higher rent if you do not want to agree yet.

**Direct debit (Lastschrift):** If you have allowed the landlord to collect the rent from your account, check your account. If the higher rent is already being collected before you have agreed, write to the landlord immediately: you have not agreed, and the payment is only made under reservation (unter Vorbehalt). Ask your bank whether you can reverse the direct debit. Get advice.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 2 BGB; BGH, Beschluss vom 30.01.2018 – VIII ZB 74/16, Rn. 13 und 21; § 276 Abs. 1, § 331 Abs. 3 ZPO.*

## Agree, agree in part, refuse

**Consent is final.** Your consent is a binding declaration. As soon as it has reached the landlord, you can no longer take it back. You also cannot cancel it like an online purchase. The new rent then counts as agreed. Even if you notice later that the rent is too high, it usually stays that way. Only in exceptional cases can a consent be challenged (anfechten), for example if the landlord deliberately deceived you. In that case, get advice immediately.

*Source: § 130 Abs. 1 BGB, § 123 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 17.10.2018 – VIII ZR 94/17; confirmed in the Urteil vom 24.04.2019 – VIII ZR 62/18, Rn. 21 und 22.*

**Tenancy started in June 2015 or later? Check the rent brake (Mietpreisbremse) first.** Was your rent perhaps already too high at the start? Then check this **before** you reply. If you agree to an increase, the new rent counts as agreed. As a rule, you can then no longer rely on the rent brake against this new rent. More on this: [Rent brake](@/mietpreisbremse.en.md).

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 28.09.2022 – VIII ZR 300/21, Leitsätze (headnotes) and Rn. 32.*

**You can agree in part.** The law says: „Soweit“ (to the extent that) you agree, you owe the higher rent. So you can, for example, agree to an increase of €30 instead of €60. You are then bound by this. The landlord can still sue for the rest. So only agree to the amount you are sure is correct. Afterwards, only pay the rent you agreed to, not the full amount demanded. Check any direct debit.

*Source: § 558b Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 20.01.2010 – VIII ZR 141/09, Rn. 19; BGH, Beschluss vom 30.01.2018 – VIII ZB 74/16, Rn. 21.*

**You do not have to give reasons for a refusal.** The law does not require you to give reasons. But you can name features that you see differently. Get advice first.

**The landlord’s forms:** The letter often comes with a form to sign. Do not sign it if you do not understand everything on it. Our letter templates only concern the rent increase and nothing else in your tenancy agreement.

## The limits of a rent increase

The new rent must not exceed two limits. The lower one applies:

1. **The local comparative rent.** In Berlin this is usually the value from the Mietspiegel, placed in the range with the Orientierungshilfe (orientation guide). Work it out with our [calculator](@/rechner.en.md).
2. **The cap (Kappungsgrenze):** In Berlin, the rent may rise by at most 15 % within three years. The calculation uses the rent that applied three years before the day from which the new rent is to apply. Increases after a modernisation (§ 559 BGB) do not count towards the 15 %. This means: the 15 % is calculated without them, and such an increase may come on top of the 15 %. Example: three years ago the net cold rent was €600. Since then it rose by €40 because of a modernisation. Then the limit is €600 + 15 % = €690, plus €40 = €730. Changes to your advance payment for service charges (Nebenkosten) have nothing to do with the net cold rent.

**Example:** Three years ago the net cold rent (Nettokaltmiete: rent without service charges and heating costs) was €600. Then it may now be at most €690 (€600 + 15 %). If the comparative rent is €660, €660 is the limit.

*Source: § 558 Abs. 1 bis 3 BGB; Berliner Kappungsgrenzenverordnung of 14.03.2023, valid until 10.05.2028 (published in the Gesetz- und Verordnungsblatt für Berlin 2023, S. 112); BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2004 – VIII ZR 177/03.*

## The waiting periods

- **1-year blocking period (Sperrfrist):** A new demand may reach you one year after the last rent increase at the earliest. The year counts from the day the last increase took effect. If the demand comes too early, it is invalid.
- **15-month waiting period (Wartefrist):** The new rent may only apply once the rent has stayed the same for 15 months. The 15 months count from the day the last increase was first due.
- Increases after a modernisation and changes to service charges (§§ 559 bis 560 BGB) do not count for either period.

*Source: § 558 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 20.01.2010 – VIII ZR 141/09, Rn. 18; BGH, Urteil vom 28.04.2004 – VIII ZR 178/03.*

## What counts for your flat?

- **Only the landlord’s fittings count.** If you installed something yourself and paid for it yourself, it does not count. This applies permanently, even if you replaced an old fitting of the landlord with permission. It is different if the landlord paid you back the costs.
- **The point in time:** The usual rent is determined for the day the demand arrives (see above). In our understanding, the condition of the flat on that day therefore also matters. Take dated photos, ideally right after receipt.
- **Area:** What counts is the real living space, not the number in the tenancy agreement. The landlord must prove the real area if you dispute it with your own measurements. If you think the number in the tenancy agreement is too large, get advice.

*Source: Berliner Mietspiegel 2026, Nr. 6.3; BGH, Urteil vom 24.10.2018 – VIII ZR 52/18; BGH, Urteil vom 18.11.2015 – VIII ZR 266/14 (Leitsatz a); BGH, Urteil vom 31.05.2017 – VIII ZR 181/16, Rn. 13.*

## Who has to prove what?

If it comes to a court case, the landlord must prove the local comparative rent. On the individual features of the Orientierungshilfe, the Landgericht Berlin (Berlin Regional Court, today Landgericht Berlin II) decided as follows: the landlord must prove plus features. You must prove minus features that you rely on. Other courts may see this differently.

So collect evidence early: tenancy agreement, handover report (Übergabeprotokoll), dated photos, invoices for things you installed yourself, letters to the landlord, names of witnesses.

*Source: BGH, Urteil vom 18.11.2020 – VIII ZR 123/20, Rn. 27; LG Berlin, Urteil vom 16.10.2018 – 67 S 150/18, Rn. 6 bis 9.*

## Terminate instead of agreeing (special right of termination, Sonderkündigungsrecht)

After a rent increase demand, you may terminate the tenancy with extraordinary notice (außerordentlich kündigen). This means: with a shorter notice period than usual. The notice must reach the landlord by the end of the second month after receipt. The tenancy then ends at the end of the month after next. Example: the notice arrives in December. Then the tenancy ends at the end of February. The rent increase then does not apply.

The text of the law says „Monat“ (month) here, not „Kalendermonat“ (calendar month). You are on the safe side if the notice arrives within two months from the day of receipt. A notice of termination must be in writing (schriftlich), that is, on paper with your signature. An email is not enough. If several people signed the tenancy agreement, as a rule all of them must sign the notice. Get advice first.

*Source: § 561 BGB, § 568 Abs. 1 BGB; BGH, Urteil vom 16.03.2005 – VIII ZR 14/04.*

## State-owned landlords

Does your flat belong to a state-owned housing company of the State of Berlin (landeseigenes Wohnungsunternehmen)? These are degewo, GESOBAU, Gewobag, HOWOGE, STADT UND LAND and WBM. Then additional commitments apply. They are mainly in the „Kooperationsvereinbarung“ (cooperation agreement) between the State of Berlin and these companies. It applies from 01.01.2024 to 31.12.2027. For berlinovo it only applies in the area „Klassisches Wohnen“ (classic housing).

**11 % in three years:** The companies belong to the „Bündnis für Wohnungsneubau und bezahlbares Wohnen“ (Alliance for New Housing Construction and Affordable Housing). The new version of the alliance of 15.07.2025 says: the state-owned companies further lower the cap (Kappungsgrenze) for increases under § 558 BGB from 15 % to 11 %. This applies for the term of the cooperation agreement. So the rent under one tenancy agreement should rise by at most 11 % in three years.

**Limit in euros per flat:** In total, the monthly rent may rise by at most

- €50 for flats up to 65 m²,
- €75 for flats up to 100 m²,
- €100 for flats up to 125 m².

For larger flats, the agreement does not name an amount. The text does not say expressly whether „insgesamt“ (in total) applies to each single increase or to the whole term.

**2.9 % per year:** The companies make sure that rents „in Summe“ (in total) do not rise by more than 2.9 % per year. This is an average across all flats of a company. It is not a limit for your individual flat.

**Law:** A Berlin law says: the state-owned companies should („sollen“) not raise the rent under § 558 BGB by more than 15 % in four years. „Sollen“ means: they are supposed to keep to this, but exceptions are possible. It is not a firm right for you.

**Affordability promise (Leistbarkeitsversprechen):** The net cold rent should not be more than 27 % of the household income. Conditions apply:

- Your income does not exceed the limits for a housing entitlement certificate (Wohnberechtigungsschein, WBS). The agreement does not say exactly which limit. The Berlin law on the state-owned companies names, for hardship cases, 220 % of the income limits from the federal Housing Promotion Act (Wohnraumförderungsgesetz, § 9 Abs. 2 WoFG).
- The flat is not too large: 45 m² for one person, 60 m² for two, 75 m² for three, 85 m² for four and 12 m² more for each additional person. If it is larger, the rent is only reduced proportionally.
- The reduction is only given **on application** (auf Antrag). According to the ombudsman office (Ombudsstelle), an informal letter or an email to your housing company is enough. Enclose proof of income and household size.
- If you receive Bürgergeld or social assistance (Sozialhilfe; SGB II or XII), the agreement promises that the rent stays affordable for you.

The companies must point out the affordability promise in every rent increase demand.

Questions about this are answered by the **Ombudsstelle für Mieterinnen und Mieter der landeseigenen Wohnungsunternehmen** (ombudsman office for tenants of the state-owned housing companies): <https://www.ombud-lwu.de> (advice in German and English). These rules are voluntary commitments and „should“ rules (Soll-Regeln). Whether and how they can be enforced in court is unclear. Get advice on this.

*Source: Kooperationsvereinbarung 2024, Präambel und Abschnitt II Nr. 4, 7 und 8; Bündnis für Wohnungsneubau und bezahlbares Wohnen, Vereinbarung vom 15.07.2025; Gesetz zur sozialen Ausrichtung und Stärkung der landeseigenen Wohnungsunternehmen, § 2 Abs. 4 und § 3; Ombudsstelle, FAQ.*
