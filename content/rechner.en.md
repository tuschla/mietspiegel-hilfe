**Does this apply to you?** The Berlin rent index (Mietspiegel) only applies to rented flats in buildings with at least 3 flats that are not price-bound (nicht preisgebunden). It does not apply to: social housing and other subsidised flats with a fixed rent, flats in one- or two-family houses and terraced houses, flats with the toilet outside the flat, and flats that only became ready for occupancy (bezugsfertig) after the period of the Mietspiegel, including through an attic conversion, an extension or the conversion of commercial premises (Mietspiegel 2026: from 2025; Mietspiegel 2024: from 2023). If you are unsure, get advice: [Help and advice](@/hilfe.en.md).

The Berlin Mietspiegel shows how high the rent for similar flats in Berlin usually is (local comparative rent, ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete). The values are net cold rent (Nettokaltmiete) per square metre per month, that is, without service charges and without heating costs.

What you need:

- Residential area (Wohnlage): simple, medium or good. The exact Wohnlage of your address is in the official street directory (Straßenverzeichnis) of the Mietspiegel. You can also find it in the Senate’s online lookup service at mietspiegel.berlin.de.
- Building age (Baualter): When was the building completed (ready for occupancy, Bezugsfertigkeit)? A modernised flat usually keeps the original building year of the house. There are exceptions, for example for an attic conversion (Dachgeschossausbau).
- Living space (Wohnfläche) in square metres. What counts is the real area, not the number in the tenancy agreement (BGH, judgment of 18.11.2015, VIII ZR 266/14). If you think the number in the tenancy agreement is too large, get advice.
- Amenities (Ausstattung): Does the flat have central heating (Sammelheizung), for example central heating for the building or a heating system for the flat (Etagenheizung) that keeps all living rooms adequately warm? Does it have a bathroom and a toilet inside the flat? Only what the landlord provided counts.

Each field has a lower value, an average value and an upper value. Where your flat lies in this range is shown by the Orientierungshilfe (orientation guide). It has five groups: bathroom, kitchen, flat, building and surroundings. In each group you count the plus features and the minus features. If there are more plus features, the group counts as plus. If there are more minus features, it counts as minus. If they are equal, the group does not count. Then the five groups are weighed against each other: one plus group and one minus group cancel each other out. Each remaining plus group moves the value up by one fifth of the distance from the average value to the upper value. Each remaining minus group moves it down by one fifth of the distance from the average value to the lower value. Example: 3 plus groups and 2 minus groups result in 1 plus group. This is not 20 % more rent.

According to the Mietspiegel, the Orientierungshilfe is not part of the „qualified“ section (qualifiziert). It is an estimation aid: a court can use it as the basis for an estimate (Mietspiegel No. 4 and No. 10). The list of features is not complete: in individual cases, other important features can count (Mietspiegel No. 10.1). The calculator only knows the features on the list. The result is therefore only an estimate. Only the official German text of the Mietspiegel is binding.
